## Instructions

When the user wants to outline a plot:

1. **Identify the Work** - Which project are they outlining?

2. **Choose a Framework** - Present options based on their needs:

| Framework | Best For | Complexity |
|-----------|----------|------------|
| Three-Act Structure | Traditional stories, beginners | Simple |
| Save the Cat (15 beats) | Commercial fiction, screenplays | Detailed |
| Hero's Journey (12 stages) | Fantasy, adventure, mythic tales | Moderate |
| Dan Harmon Story Circle | Character-driven, TV episodes | Simple |
| Seven-Point Structure | Fast-paced, focused stories | Moderate |
| Fichtean Curve | Thriller, action, tension-driven | Moderate |
| Freytag's Pyramid | Literary fiction, tragedies | Classic |
| Kishōtenketsu | Slice-of-life, low-conflict stories | Simple |
| Pixar Story Spine | Concept development, summaries | Simple |
| Snowflake Method | Complex novels, planners | Detailed |
| 27-Chapter Method | Novels with clear milestones | Detailed |

3. **Gather Core Elements**:
 - Protagonist and their goal
 - Main conflict/antagonist
 - Stakes (what happens if they fail?)
 - Theme (what's the story really about?)

4. **Generate Plot Outline** in the work file or as separate document

## Framework Templates

### 1. Three-Act Structure (Classic)

```markdown
## 🎯 Plot Outline: Three-Act Structure

### Act I: Setup (0-25%)

#### Opening Image
[First impression of the world/character]

#### Ordinary World
[Status quo before the story begins]

#### Inciting Incident
[The event that disrupts everything]

#### Debate/Refusal
[Character hesitates or resists the call]

#### First Plot Point (25%)
[Character commits to the journey - no turning back]

---

### Act II: Confrontation (25-75%)

#### Rising Action
[Character pursues goal, faces obstacles]

#### First Pinch Point (37%)
[Antagonist shows their power]

#### Midpoint (50%)
[Major revelation or reversal - raises stakes]

#### Second Pinch Point (62%)
[Antagonist closes in]

#### All Is Lost (75%)
[Lowest point - seems impossible to win]

#### Dark Night of the Soul
[Character confronts their flaw/fear]

---

### Act III: Resolution (75-100%)

#### Second Plot Point
[New information or insight enables final push]

#### Climax (88%)
[Final confrontation with antagonist]

#### Resolution
[New status quo established]

#### Final Image
[Mirror of opening - shows transformation]
```

### 2. Save the Cat (Blake Snyder Beat Sheet - 15 Beats)

```markdown
## 🎯 Plot Outline: Save the Cat

| Beat | % | Target Words* | Description |
|------|---|---------------|-------------|
| **Opening Image** | 0% | 0-1% | [Snapshot of "before" - tone, mood, protagonist's world] |
| **Theme Stated** | 5% | ~5% | [Theme hinted at, often in dialogue - what the story is really about] |
| **Setup** | 1-10% | 1-10% | [Establish world, introduce characters, show stakes, plant setups] |
| **Catalyst** | 10% | ~10% | [Life-changing event that kicks off the story] |
| **Debate** | 10-20% | 10-20% | [Should they act? Weighing the decision] |
| **Break into Two** | 20% | ~20% | [Protagonist chooses to act - enters the "upside-down world"] |
| **B Story** | 22% | ~22% | [Subplot begins - often love interest or friendship that carries theme] |
| **Fun and Games** | 20-50% | 20-50% | [Promise of the premise - why we bought the ticket] |
| **Midpoint** | 50% | ~50% | [False victory OR false defeat - stakes raise, no going back] |
| **Bad Guys Close In** | 50-75% | 50-75% | [External pressure + internal doubts combine] |
| **All Is Lost** | 75% | ~75% | [Whiff of death - mentor dies, all seems hopeless] |
| **Dark Night of Soul** | 75-80% | 75-80% | [Wallowing in hopelessness before the breakthrough] |
| **Break into Three** | 80% | ~80% | [Solution found through synthesis of A and B stories] |
| **Finale** | 80-99% | 80-99% | [Execute the plan - confront villain - prove transformation] |
| **Final Image** | 100% | 100% | [Proof of change - opposite of opening image] |

*For 80,000-word novel
```

### 3. Hero's Journey (Joseph Campbell - 12 Stages)

```markdown
## 🎯 Plot Outline: Hero's Journey

### ACT I: DEPARTURE

#### 1. Ordinary World
[Hero's normal life before the adventure begins]
- Who is the hero?
- What's their everyday life like?
- What's missing or flawed?

#### 2. Call to Adventure
[Challenge, problem, or quest appears]
- What disrupts their world?
- What opportunity/threat emerges?

#### 3. Refusal of the Call
[Hero hesitates, shows fear or reluctance]
- Why do they resist?
- What are they afraid of losing?

#### 4. Meeting the Mentor
[Guidance, training, or magical gift received]
- Who helps prepare them?
- What tool/knowledge do they receive?

#### 5. Crossing the Threshold
[Hero commits to the journey, enters special world]
- Point of no return
- Physical or metaphorical boundary crossed

---

### ACT II: INITIATION

#### 6. Tests, Allies, Enemies
[Hero learns rules of special world]
- Who joins them?
- Who opposes them?
- What challenges do they face?

#### 7. Approach to Inmost Cave
[Preparing for major ordeal]
- What's at the heart of the special world?
- Final preparations

#### 8. Ordeal (Supreme Ordeal)
[Major crisis - death and rebirth moment]
- Biggest fear confronted
- Near-death or actual death (metaphorical)
- Transformation begins

#### 9. Reward (Seizing the Sword)
[Hero takes the prize/treasure]
- What did they gain?
- Celebration or brief respite

---

### ACT III: RETURN

#### 10. The Road Back
[Hero is pursued or must return]
- Urgency to return home
- Chase or ticking clock

#### 11. Resurrection (Final Test)
[Climax - hero faces death again, purified]
- Final confrontation
- Complete transformation
- Old self dies, new self emerges

#### 12. Return with the Elixir
[Hero brings gift back to ordinary world]
- What wisdom/treasure do they share?
- How has their world changed?
```

### 4. Dan Harmon Story Circle (8 Steps)

Based on the Hero's Journey but simplified for episodic storytelling.

```markdown
## 🎯 Plot Outline: Story Circle

The circle moves: Comfort → Desire → Unfamiliar → Adaptation → Find → Take → Return → Change

```
 1. COMFORT
 (You)
 8. CHANGE ↗ ↘ 2. NEED
 (Changed) (Need)
 \ /
 7. RETURN ← ○ → 3. GO
 (Return) (Go)
 / \
 6. TAKE ↙ ↖ 4. SEARCH
 (Take) (Search)
 5. FIND
 (Find)
```

### Top Half (Comfort Zone - Order)
1. **YOU** - A character is in a zone of comfort
2. **NEED** - But they want something
3. **GO** - They enter an unfamiliar situation
4. **SEARCH** - Adapt to it / search for what they want

### Bottom Half (Unknown - Chaos)
5. **FIND** - Get what they wanted
6. **TAKE** - Pay a heavy price for it
7. **RETURN** - Return to their familiar situation
8. **CHANGE** - Having changed

### Detailed Breakdown

| Step | Question | Your Story |
|------|----------|------------|
| 1. You | Who is your protagonist in their comfort zone? | |
| 2. Need | What do they want or need? | |
| 3. Go | What unfamiliar situation do they enter? | |
| 4. Search | How do they adapt and search? | |
| 5. Find | What do they find (often not what expected)? | |
| 6. Take | What price do they pay? | |
| 7. Return | How do they return to the familiar? | |
| 8. Change | How have they fundamentally changed? | |
```

### 5. Seven-Point Structure (Dan Wells)

Start with the ending, work backward.

```markdown
## 🎯 Plot Outline: Seven-Point Structure

**Key Principle:** Plot points alternate between highs (↑) and lows (↓)

| Point | Position | State | Description |
|-------|----------|-------|-------------|
| 1. **Hook** | Opening | ↓ | [Opposite of Resolution - character at starting state] |
| 2. **Plot Turn 1** | ~15% | ↑ | [Call to adventure - conflict introduced, world changes] |
| 3. **Pinch Point 1** | ~25% | ↓ | [Pressure applied - something goes wrong, stakes raised] |
| 4. **Midpoint** | 50% | ↑ | [Character moves from reaction to ACTION - takes control] |
| 5. **Pinch Point 2** | ~75% | ↓ | [More pressure - jaws of defeat, seems hopeless] |
| 6. **Plot Turn 2** | ~85% | ↑ | [Final piece of puzzle - "aha!" moment, victory possible] |
| 7. **Resolution** | End | ↑/↓ | [Climax and ending - opposite of Hook, transformation complete] |

### Working Backward Method

1. Start with **Resolution** - How does it end? What state is the character in?
2. Then **Hook** - What's the OPPOSITE? That's where they start.
3. Then **Midpoint** - What's the turning point between these states?
4. Then **Plot Turns** - What gets them INTO trouble (1) and OUT of trouble (2)?
5. Then **Pinch Points** - What applies pressure and raises stakes?

### Your Seven Points

| Point | Event |
|-------|-------|
| Resolution | |
| Hook | |
| Midpoint | |
| Plot Turn 1 | |
| Plot Turn 2 | |
| Pinch Point 1 | |
| Pinch Point 2 | |
```

### 6. Fichtean Curve (Crisis-Driven)

Best for thrillers, action, and tension-heavy stories.

```markdown
## 🎯 Plot Outline: Fichtean Curve

**Key Feature:** Starts with rising action immediately - no slow build.

```
 CLIMAX
 /\
 / \
 Crisis 4 / \
 /\ / \
 Crisis 3 / \ / \
 /\ / X \
 Crisis 2 / \/ \
 /\ / \
 Crisis 1 / \/ \
 /\ / \
 / \/ \
INCITING / \ RESOLUTION
 ───── ─────
```

### Structure

**No Traditional Setup** - Jump into action quickly

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Inciting Incident** | Story starts with conflict already brewing or exploding |
| **Rising Crises** | Series of escalating conflicts, each bigger than the last |
| **Crisis 1** | First major obstacle - raises stakes |
| **Crisis 2** | Bigger complication - things get worse |
| **Crisis 3** | Major setback - seems impossible |
| **Crisis 4** | Ultimate challenge before climax |
| **Climax** | Peak of tension - everything comes together |
| **Falling Action** | Quick resolution - don't linger |

### Crisis Escalation Table

| Crisis | Stakes | Character State | Obstacle |
|--------|--------|-----------------|----------|
| 1 | | | |
| 2 | | | |
| 3 | | | |
| 4 | | | |
| Climax | | | |
```

### 7. Freytag's Pyramid (5 Acts - Classical)

Best for literary fiction, tragedies, and character studies.

```markdown
## 🎯 Plot Outline: Freytag's Pyramid (1863)

```
 CLIMAX
 /\
 / \
 / \
 RISING / \ FALLING
 ACTION / \ ACTION
 / \
 / \
 / \
 EXPOSITION RESOLUTION
 ───────── ─────────
 │
 DÉNOUEMENT
```

### Five Parts

#### 1. Exposition (Introduction)
- Setting, characters, and situation established
- Normal world before conflict
- Necessary backstory revealed
- **Ends with:** Inciting incident

#### 2. Rising Action (Complication)
- Series of events building tension
- Protagonist pursues goal
- Obstacles and setbacks
- Character development
- **Ends with:** Approach to climax

#### 3. Climax (Turning Point)
- Peak of emotional intensity
- Major confrontation or decision
- Everything changes after this moment
- In tragedy: protagonist's fatal error

#### 4. Falling Action (Reversal)
- Consequences of climax unfold
- Unraveling of complications
- Movement toward resolution
- Final conflicts resolved

#### 5. Dénouement (Resolution/Catastrophe)
- New equilibrium established
- Loose ends tied up
- In tragedy: catastrophe/death
- In comedy: marriage/celebration
- Final image or moral
```

### 8. Kishōtenketsu (4 Acts - No-Conflict Structure)

Japanese/Chinese structure - ideal for slice-of-life, contemplative stories.

```markdown
## 🎯 Plot Outline: Kishōtenketsu

**Key Feature:** Story without central conflict - focuses on twist/surprise.

| Act | Japanese | Meaning | Purpose |
|-----|----------|---------|---------|
| **Ki** | 起 | Introduction | Establish characters and setting |
| **Shō** | 承 | Development | Develop what was introduced, deepen understanding |
| **Ten** | 転 | Twist | Unexpected element - NOT conflict, but shift in perspective |
| **Ketsu** | 結 | Conclusion | Reconcile the twist with what came before |

### The Four Acts

#### Ki (起) - Introduction (25%)
[Introduce the world and characters naturally]
- Who are the characters?
- What is their situation?
- What's normal life like?

#### Shō (承) - Development (25%)
[Continue and deepen the introduction - no conflict yet]
- Show more of the characters' lives
- Develop relationships
- Build familiarity

#### Ten (転) - Twist (25%)
[Introduce surprising element that recontextualizes everything]
- NOT necessarily conflict
- A revelation, discovery, or shift
- Something that changes how we see what came before
- The "ah-ha" moment

#### Ketsu (結) - Conclusion (25%)
[Bring elements together in new understanding]
- How does the twist change things?
- New equilibrium or perspective
- Gentle resolution

### Example Structure

| Act | Event | Emotion |
|-----|-------|---------|
| Ki | [Setup] | Familiarity |
| Shō | [Deepening] | Connection |
| Ten | [Surprise] | Wonder/Realization |
| Ketsu | [Synthesis] | Satisfaction |

**Best For:** Slice-of-life, meditative stories, Studio Ghibli-style narratives, stories about everyday moments
```

### 9. Pixar Story Spine

Quick summary formula for any story.

```markdown
## 🎯 Plot Outline: Pixar Story Spine

Complete each line to create your story summary:

**Once upon a time** ___________________________________
[Establish the world and protagonist]

**Every day** ___________________________________
[The routine, the status quo]

**But one day** ___________________________________
[The inciting incident - everything changes]

**Because of that** ___________________________________
[First consequence]

**Because of that** ___________________________________
[Second consequence - escalation]

**Because of that** ___________________________________
[Third consequence - things get serious]

**Until finally** ___________________________________
[The climax - confrontation]

**And ever since then** ___________________________________
[The new normal - transformation]

**The moral is** ___________________________________
[Theme statement - optional]

---

### Example (Finding Nemo)

**Once upon a time** there was a clownfish named Marlin who was overprotective of his son.

**Every day** Marlin would worry about Nemo and restrict his freedom.

**But one day** Nemo was captured by a scuba diver.

**Because of that** Marlin had to leave the safety of the reef to find him.

**Because of that** Marlin met Dory and faced his fears crossing the ocean.

**Because of that** Marlin learned to trust others and let go of control.

**Until finally** Marlin found Nemo and had to trust him to save the day.

**And ever since then** Marlin learned to give Nemo freedom while still being a loving father.

**The moral is** sometimes you have to let go of those you love to truly protect them.
```

### 10. Snowflake Method (Randy Ingermanson)

For complex novels - expand from simple to complex.

```markdown
## 🎯 Plot Outline: Snowflake Method

### Step 1: One-Sentence Summary (1 hour)
Write your story in one sentence (15-25 words, no character names):
> ___________________________________

### Step 2: One-Paragraph Summary (1 hour)
Expand to five sentences:
1. Setup and first disaster
2. First quarter developments and second disaster
3. Midpoint and third disaster
4. Third quarter and final disaster
5. Ending

> ___________________________________

### Step 3: Character Summaries (1 hour per character)
For each major character:
- Name:
- One-sentence summary:
- Motivation (what do they want abstractly?):
- Goal (what do they want concretely?):
- Conflict (what prevents them?):
- Epiphany (what do they learn?):
- One-paragraph summary:

### Step 4: Expand Paragraph to Page (several hours)
Turn each sentence from Step 2 into a paragraph. End each paragraph (except last) with a disaster.

### Step 5: Character Synopses (1-2 days)
One-page synopsis for each major character showing their story arc.

### Step 6: Four-Page Synopsis (1 week)
Expand the one-page plot to four pages.

### Step 7: Character Charts (1 week)
Full character profiles for everyone.

### Step 8: Scene List (1 week)
List every scene with:
- POV character
- What happens
- How many pages

### Step 9: Scene Descriptions (optional)
Multi-paragraph descriptions of each scene.

### Step 10: First Draft
Write!
```

### 11. 27-Chapter Method (Kat O'Keeffe)

Structured novel outline with clear milestones.

```markdown
## 🎯 Plot Outline: 27-Chapter Method

### ACT I (Chapters 1-7)

| Ch | Beat | Description |
|----|------|-------------|
| 1 | **Opening Image** | [Hook, world, protagonist's normal] |
| 2 | **Setup** | [Expand world, stakes, want vs. need] |
| 3 | **Theme Stated** | [Theme hinted, foreshadowing] |
| 4 | **Catalyst** | [Inciting incident - world disrupted] |
| 5 | **Debate** | [Hesitation, weighing options] |
| 6 | **B-Story Intro** | [Subplot begins, often relationship] |
| 7 | **Break into Act II** | [Decision made, crosses threshold] |

### ACT II-A (Chapters 8-14)

| Ch | Beat | Description |
|----|------|-------------|
| 8 | **Fun and Games** | [Promise of premise begins] |
| 9 | **Ascending Action** | [Progress toward goal] |
| 10 | **B-Story Development** | [Subplot deepens] |
| 11 | **First Pinch Point** | [Antagonist shows power] |
| 12 | **Approaching Midpoint** | [Tension builds] |
| 13 | **Midpoint Build** | [Setup for revelation] |
| 14 | **Midpoint** | [False victory/defeat, stakes raise] |

### ACT II-B (Chapters 15-21)

| Ch | Beat | Description |
|----|------|-------------|
| 15 | **Post-Midpoint Fallout** | [Dealing with midpoint consequences] |
| 16 | **Bad Guys Close In** | [External pressures mount] |
| 17 | **B-Story Complication** | [Subplot conflict] |
| 18 | **Second Pinch Point** | [Antagonist strikes again] |
| 19 | **All Is Lost Setup** | [Things crumble] |
| 20 | **All Is Lost** | [Rock bottom, whiff of death] |
| 21 | **Dark Night of Soul** | [Despair before breakthrough] |

### ACT III (Chapters 22-27)

| Ch | Beat | Description |
|----|------|-------------|
| 22 | **Break into Act III** | [New plan, insight gained] |
| 23 | **Gathering the Team** | [Preparation for finale] |
| 24 | **Finale Setup** | [Approach final confrontation] |
| 25 | **Climax Part 1** | [Confrontation begins] |
| 26 | **Climax Part 2** | [Resolution of conflict] |
| 27 | **Final Image** | [New normal, transformation shown] |
```

## Choosing a Framework

| If you're writing... | Try... |
|---------------------|--------|
| Fantasy epic with chosen one | Hero's Journey |
| Commercial thriller | Save the Cat or Fichtean Curve |
| TV episode or web serial | Story Circle |
| Literary character study | Freytag's Pyramid |
| Slice-of-life or quiet story | Kishōtenketsu |
| Fast-paced action | Fichtean Curve |
| Complex novel, need detail | Snowflake or 27-Chapter |
| Still figuring out the concept | Pixar Story Spine |
